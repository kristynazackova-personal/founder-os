# Stage 4 - Solutions: what to consider, and how to score it

Researched September 2026. Sources at the bottom.

## What her doc already fixes

Brainstorm solutions for the chosen pains, score them, choose one to three,
say why, then write three things v1 is NOT.

| Column in the doc | Scale |
|---|---|
| Solution idea | text |
| How well it solves the pain | L / M / H |
| How hard to build | L / M / H |

That is a value-versus-effort matrix, which is the standard simple form: a
2x2 of high/low value against high/low effort.

## What the literature adds

**RICE is the fuller version:** (Reach x Impact x Confidence) / Effort, from
Intercom. Reach is users affected in a defined window. Impact is a fixed
ladder - 3 massive, 2 high, 1 medium, 0.5 low, 0.25 minimal. Confidence is a
percentage over your own reach and impact estimates. Effort is person-months.

**ICE drops reach and the effort divisor**, which makes it faster and cruder,
and it suits early-stage triage where speed matters more than rigour.

**At this stage, ICE or value/effort is the right tool, not RICE.** RICE
needs a reach number and an effort estimate in person-months. A founder who
has not yet run the interviews from stage 3 has neither, and inventing them
produces a defensible-looking score built on two guesses.

**The column worth stealing from RICE is confidence.** It is the only one
that records how much the row is worth trusting, and it costs nothing to
estimate. Without it, a scored table reads as researched when half of it is
assumption.

**Weighted scoring exists for when the criteria are not the standard ones.**
Unlike ICE and RICE, weighted scoring is fully customisable: pick four to six
criteria that reflect what actually drives the decision, weight them, and the
weights must sum to 100%. The consistent advice is that criteria should
reflect strategic objectives rather than a borrowed formula - which is the
same instruction her doc gives in its own words.

**Four to six criteria is the practical ceiling.** More than that and the
scoring stops being done.

## Candidate columns, with when each earns its place

| Column | Scale | Earns its place when |
|---|---|---|
| Solution idea | text | Always. |
| Which pain it solves | reference to a stage 3 row | Always - a solution not tied to a scored pain is how scope creeps back in. |
| How well it solves it | L / M / H | Always. Her default. |
| How hard to build | L / M / H | Always. Her default. |
| Confidence | L / M / H | Always - it is the cheapest honesty column there is. |
| Reach | number or S / M / L | Only when stage 2 produced a real size estimate; otherwise it is a guess dressed as a number. |
| Time to first version | days / weeks / months | When the goal's six-month picture is aggressive. |
| Depends on | text | When two ideas are actually one sequenced thing. |

## What this does NOT settle

- Nothing in the literature says how many candidate solutions is right. Her
  doc says one to three chosen, and is silent on how many to brainstorm.
- "What v1 is NOT" has no equivalent in the scoring frameworks at all. It is
  hers, and it is the only part that limits scope rather than ordering it -
  the prompt should treat it as required, not optional.
