# Stage 2 - Target user: what to consider, and how to score it

Researched September 2026. Sources at the bottom.

## What her doc already fixes

Three columns, MECE grouping, one chosen segment with a reason, plus open
assumptions to test. Her note: weight pay-strength less when optimising for
impact over revenue, but do not ignore it, because the thing still has to
sustain itself.

| Column in the doc | Scale |
|---|---|
| Size of market | S / M / L |
| Pay-strength / willingness | L / M / H |
| Already solved elsewhere | Y / N |

## What the literature adds

**A beachhead is not the biggest segment, it is the most desperate one.**
The recurring criterion is urgency: pick where early customers are desperate
for a solution and willing to pay, and where the segment is small enough to
stay intimate with while being large enough to grow into. Skipping this is
the named failure - founders spread thin, burn cash, and learn slowly.

**Early adopters are their own segment, not a trait sprinkled across others.**
People who adopt a new category early behave differently from those who wait
for it to mature, so "early adopters of X" is often a legitimate MECE group
rather than a qualifier on another row.

**Reachability belongs in the table.** ICP rubrics consistently score how
easy a segment is to reach and convert, not only how attractive it is. A
segment you cannot get in front of scores well and still cannot be sold to -
this is the column founders most often leave out.

**Urgency and budget are separate columns.** ICP scoring separates "can they
pay" from "do they need it now". Her pay-strength covers the first. The
second is missing from the doc and is usually the stronger predictor at this
stage.

**Score on a consistent scale, then shortlist.** The common shape is 1-10 per
attribute to produce a short list, or S/M/L-style buckets when the numbers
would be false precision. At a handful of segments and no data, buckets are
the honest choice, which is what her doc already uses.

**Validation has a number attached.** The repeated instruction is to talk to
15-20 prospects in the top candidate segment before committing. That is worth
carrying into the assumptions block, because "test the assumption" without a
sample size is not actionable.

## Candidate columns, with when each earns its place

| Column | Scale | Earns its place when |
|---|---|---|
| Size of market | S / M / L | Always. Her default. |
| Pay-strength | L / M / H | The goal mentions revenue, sustainability or a price. Drop it if the stated goal is impact-only, per her own example. |
| Urgency of the pain | L / M / H | Almost always - it predicts early traction better than size. |
| Already solved elsewhere | Y / N | Always. Her default. |
| Reachability | L / M / H | Whenever the founder has no existing audience in that segment. |
| Early-adopter tendency | L / M / H | Consumer or developer products, new categories. |
| You can reach them this week | Y / N | When the goal's six-month picture depends on talking to users soon. |

## What this does NOT settle

- No source gives a defensible way to size a consumer segment without data.
  S/M/L is a judgement, and the tool must ask for it rather than assert it.
- "Already solved elsewhere" is ambiguous: solved well, or solved badly by
  someone with distribution. The generated prompt should ask which.
